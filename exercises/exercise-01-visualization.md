## Getting started

The PReSto platform is at https://paleopresto.org/. You can go there now. Start by selecting either Holocene or Common Era, and finding a reconstruction that interests you. Then select "Visualizer". You're not sure which to choose, [LMR](https://paleopresto.org/visualizer.html?dataset=lmr) is a good one to play with.

### Key Platform Features to Know

Before diving into the tasks, take a few minutes to locate these controls in the visualization platform with your chosen reconstruction:

- **Reconstruction selector** (top dropdown): switches between published reconstructions and methods (there may only be one depending on which reconstruction it says)
- **Variable selector**: choose the climate variable you want to display (e.g., surface air temperature) (again not all reconstructions have more than 1)
- **Time slider**: scrub through time or type a specific year/decade
- **Time series panel**: click any grid cell on the map to pull up a time series for that location
- **Reginonal means**: Click the layers stack in the top right of the map and turn on the regions. Then click a region to get a regional average timeseries.

## Task 1: Holocene Comparison

The Holocene Conundrum is alive and well, and many things about the early to mid Holocene, including whether it was warmer or cooler than the late Holoene, remain contested. Two major, data-assimilation-based  reconstruction, the **Last Glacial Maximum Reanalysis (LGMR, Osman et al., 2021)** and the **Holocene Data Assimilation product (HoloceneDA, Erb et al., 2022)**, take different approaches and produce strikingly different spatial patterns in the early Holocene, particularly around 8,000–6,000 years before present. Let's explore the results. 

### Steps

1. Open the PReSto visualization platform: [paleopresto.org](https://paleopresto.org/)
2. Select the **LGMR** reconstruction. Set the variable to **surface air temperature (TAS)**.
3. Navigate to approximately **6,000 years BP**. Take a screenshot of the global map.
4. Click on a grid cell in the **North America/Laurentide region**. Zoom in on the time axis so that only the Holocene is visible. Take another screenshot. 
5. Now do the same for the **HoloceneDA** reconstruction. You probably want to open another window or tab.
6. Take the same two screenshots (map + time series for the same grid cell).
7. Upload all four screenshots to your slide. Add a sentence or two describing the most striking differences you notice between the two reconstructions, both in their spatial patterns and temporal evolution. Pay attention to the temperature scale.

> **Discussion prompt:** What the heck is going on? Based on your expertise, which seems more realistic? Based on your understanding of data assimilation methodologies, what different choices might these authors have made to lead to these differences? Consider digging into the methodologies of each study to try to get at the differences.

## Task 2: A Volcanic Eruption

The 1815 eruption of Mount Tambora is one of the largest in the historical record and produced a well-documented global cooling signal. Here you will examine how that signal appears across different Common Era reconstructions.

### Steps

1. Select the **LMR 2.1** reconstruction. Set the variable to **surface air temperature**.
2. Navigate to **1816** (the "Year Without a Summer").
3. Screenshot the global anomaly map.
4. Click on a grid cell in **Europe** and screenshot the time series, zooming in to the 1600-2000 window.
5. Switch to at least one other Common Era reconstruction (e.g., **PHYDA**) and repeat steps 3–4 for the same locations.
6. Upload your screenshots and add a brief note: does the spatial structure of the cooling look the same across reconstructions? Where does it differ?

> **Discussion prompt:** What would cause two reconstructions to agree on the timing of Tambora cooling but disagree on its spatial extent or magnitude?

Now check out the [comparison tool](https://paleopresto.org/comparison.html?dataset=lmr). You can also find it at the bottom of the Common Era reconstructions page. Use this to explore changes in hydroclimate in 1816 by comparing a DA product (LMR or PHYDA) to a drought atlas product (e.g., OWDA or NADA). Upload some screenshots with this tool. 

 > **Discussion prompt:** The DA products show a markedly different pattern in hydroclimate in Europe in 1816 than OWDA does. Why might this have happened?

## Task 3: Choose Your Own Event

Pick a climate event or period you are personally interested in. It can be anything covered by one of the available reconstructions. Some ideas if you need a starting point:

- Younger Dryas (~12,900–11,700 BP)
- Medieval Climate Anomaly (~950–1250 CE)
- Little Ice Age (~1300–1850 CE)
- 8.2 ka event
- 4.2 ka "event"
- A specific historical drought or flood event

### Steps

1. Choose a reconstruction and time period relevant to your event.
2. Explore the spatial and temporal structure of the climate signal.
3. Capture 2–3 screenshots that best illustrate what you found.
4. Upload to your slide with a title and a short description: what event did you pick, what reconstruction did you use, and what does the spatial/temporal structure look like?

We'll briefly share these out at the end of the exercise — be ready to spend ~2 minutes walking the group through what you found.
